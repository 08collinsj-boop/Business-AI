Privacy Notice

Version 1.0 · Effective 27 September 2026

Pilot identity notice. Business AI is currently an invitation-only UK Pilot. The service provider's final legal/trading name, geographic address and direct contact email are not yet published. Those details must be completed before any paid public launch. Pilot users should use the support route supplied with their invitation.

1. Roles

Business AI has two main data-protection roles. For account administration, billing, service security, operational logging and Pilot support, the Business AI operator acts as a controller. For customer enquiry data processed on behalf of a business, that business is normally the controller and Business AI acts as its processor under the Data Processing Agreement.

2. Information we handle

Depending on how the service is used, Business AI may handle account email and authentication records; business profile and configuration information; customer names, contact details and enquiry messages; leads, actions and booking requests; approved Business Knowledge files and extracted facts; marketing drafts, publishing records and connected social-account identifiers; billing/subscription metadata; security and audit events; and Pilot feedback.

Raw payment-card information should not be entered into Business AI. Stripe handles payment-card processing for supported checkout flows.

3. Why we use personal information

For Business AI's own controller activities, information is used to provide and administer accounts and subscriptions, authenticate users, protect the service, prevent abuse, respond to support requests, maintain audit records and meet legal obligations. Depending on the activity, the lawful basis may be performance of a contract, legitimate interests in operating and securing the service, or compliance with a legal obligation. Where consent is specifically required, it will be requested separately.

4. Customer enquiries

When a person uses a business's public Business AI enquiry page, their message and any contact details they provide are collected for that business. The business determines the purpose and lawful basis for that customer relationship. Business AI processes the information to generate responses, capture the enquiry, create leads/actions/bookings where configured, support human handover and make the information available to authorised members of that business.

If you are a customer contacting a business, requests about access, correction, deletion or objection should normally be made to that business first. Business AI provides technical tools to help the business respond.

5. AI processing

Customer messages and relevant approved business context may be sent to an AI provider to generate a response or marketing content. Business AI is designed to support human handover and does not intend to make solely automated decisions that have legal or similarly significant effects on people.

6. Who receives information

Business AI uses service providers for hosting, database/authentication/storage, AI processing, billing and optional social publishing. The current list is available in the Sub-processor Notice. Access is limited to what is needed for the relevant service.

7. International transfers

Some service providers may process information outside the UK. Where UK restricted-transfer rules apply, Business AI must use an available lawful transfer mechanism and complete any required transfer assessment. The exact provider arrangements are reviewed as part of the Pilot's launch controls.

8. Retention

Business AI includes business-configurable data-lifecycle review settings. The Pilot defaults currently use a 365-day lead review period and a 730-day audit review period, but these are review settings rather than a promise of automatic deletion. Account, billing, security and legal records are kept only as long as reasonably needed for the relevant purpose or legal requirement. At the end of processor services, customer data is handled under the DPA.

9. Security

The Pilot uses authenticated tenant membership, role-based access, database row-level security, server-side secrets, encryption for supported social tokens, audit logging and private knowledge storage. No online system can guarantee absolute security, and users must also protect their accounts.

10. Your rights

Depending on the circumstances, UK data-protection law may give you rights of access, rectification, erasure, restriction, objection and data portability, and rights relating to automated decision-making. You may also complain to the Information Commissioner's Office. For customer-enquiry data, contact the relevant business first; for Business AI account/controller data, use the Pilot support route supplied to your business.

11. Storage on your device

Business AI uses essential browser storage for authentication and temporary app functions. The current Pilot does not intentionally deploy advertising or behavioural-analytics trackers. See the Storage Notice.

12. Changes

Material changes to this notice are versioned. Where appropriate, account users will be shown the new version in the product.
